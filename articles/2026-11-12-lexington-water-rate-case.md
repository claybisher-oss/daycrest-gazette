Kentucky American Water, which serves most of Fayette County and parts of 13 surrounding counties, is asking state regulators to let it collect about $17.7 million more each year. The company says a typical household would pay about $8 more a month. Lexington has joined the case to oppose it, [WKYT reports](https://www.wkyt.com/2026/11/10/lexington-opposes-kentucky-american-water-rate-increase-request/).

## Both sides

- **The company** says the money supports about $108 million in system improvements planned for next year, and that a yearly increase isn't typical but is needed this time.
- **The city** calls it the third request in three years and the second in twelve months. An accountant testifying for Lexington, the Attorney General and Georgetown disputes the company's numbers. Mayor Linda Gorton told regulators in October that bills have risen more than 60% since 2019, [WEKU reported](https://www.weku.org/2026-10-14/kentucky-american-water-rate-increase-is-an-outrage-to-our-people-gorton-tells-psc).

## How a rate case works

![How a utility rate request moves through the state](img/2026-11-12-rate-case.svg)

A private utility can't simply raise its prices. It has to ask the **Kentucky Public Service Commission (PSC)** and prove its costs. Others, like the city and the Attorney General, can **intervene**: they become official parties, question the company's numbers and bring their own experts. The PSC then approves the request, approves less, or denies it.

One wrinkle this time: the company says new rates could take effect **on an interim basis in December**, with any difference refunded after the final decision.

## Why it matters

Water is the one utility no household can do without. If the interim rates start in December, bills rise before anyone knows the final number, so the PSC's eventual decision will decide whether customers see a refund.
