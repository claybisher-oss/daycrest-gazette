After decades tied up at a pier, one of the most famous ships in American history has a new job. The SS United States was sunk off the coast of Florida this week, NPR reports, to become the largest artificial reef in the world.

## The ship

![The SS United States beside the Titanic, to scale](img/2026-11-12-ship-lengths.svg)

Entering service in 1952, the *United States* was built to be the fastest and safest liner afloat, and on her first voyage she crossed the Atlantic faster than any liner before or since. That record still stands. She was also bigger than the Titanic, at about 990 feet long. Jet travel ended the age of the great liners, and she was retired in 1969 and spent most of the years since docked and slowly decaying while preservation groups tried to save her.

## Why sink a ship on purpose?

A cleaned-out steel hull on the sea floor quickly becomes a home for sea life. Coral, sponges and algae grow on the steel; small fish shelter in the structure; bigger fish follow. Coastal communities sink old ships to create fishing and diving spots and to take pressure off natural reefs. Before sinking, the ship is stripped of fuel, oils and other pollutants.

## Why it matters

It's a bittersweet ending. A ship that couldn't be saved as a museum will still be visited, by divers instead of tourists, and it gives the ocean a new reef.
