The holiday toy drive run by the Marine Corps Reserve in Lexington is gearing up, and organizers say this year's need is the biggest they've seen.

## The numbers

Last year the Lexington chapter gave out more than 16,000 new toys to about 7,000 children in the area. This year it hopes to reach even more families. The program's local coordinator, Mattea Clark, told LEX 18 that times are hard for many households this year.

## Where the gaps are

Two age groups are hardest to fill:

- **Babies and toddlers (0–4).** Donors tend to shop for the elementary years.
- **Teenagers (13 and up).** Teens are the most often overlooked. Gift cards, books, sports gear, art supplies and games are the usual go-tos.

Every toy has to be **new and unwrapped**, so families get something that's truly theirs.

## How to help

There are two ways in: drop a new toy in a collection box, or volunteer to help sort and hand out toys as Christmas gets closer. The local Toys for Tots website lists the current drop-off sites and volunteer sign-ups.

## Why it matters

A present on Christmas morning is a small thing that isn't small to a child. Filling the two gaps the organizers named, a toddler toy or a gift for a teenager, is the most useful way to give this year.
