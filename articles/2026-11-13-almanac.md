Mid-November is when the shortening days start to feel personal. Here is where the sky and the calendar stand this Friday in Central Kentucky.

## Sun and Moon

- **Sunrise:** 7:16 a.m. Eastern in Lexington.
- **Sunset:** 5:28 p.m.
- **Daylight:** 10 hours 11 minutes, and we are still losing roughly two minutes a day until the winter solstice on December 21.
- **Moon:** a young waxing crescent, about four days past new and roughly 15% lit. It sets in the early evening, so look low in the southwest just after dusk. Today's clouds will probably hide it; Saturday's clear sky is the better bet.

(Times are computed with the standard NOAA sunrise equation for 38.04° N, 84.50° W; expect a minute or so of difference from other sources.)

## The church year

We are in the long Season after Pentecost, in Year A of the Revised Common Lectionary. This Sunday is Proper 28, and Advent begins on November 29, which also starts a new lectionary year.

The Church of England remembers **Charles Simeon** today (the Episcopal Church keeps him on November 12). Simeon was a Cambridge pastor who died in 1836 after more than fifty years at Holy Trinity Church. He started out unwelcome: for years parishioners locked their pew doors against him. He stayed, preached the plain sense of the Bible text, and trained a generation of clergy to do the same. His sermon outlines, collected in many volumes, are still in print.

## On this day in Kentucky

**Louis D. Brandeis** was born in Louisville on November 13, 1856. He became one of the most influential justices to sit on the U.S. Supreme Court, serving from 1916 to 1939. Before that, he co-wrote the 1890 essay "The Right to Privacy," which shaped American law, and pioneered the "Brandeis brief," a legal argument built mostly on social facts and data rather than precedent alone. The University of Louisville's law school bears his name.

## Why it matters

Small rhythms (sunrise, moonrise, saints' days, birthdays of notable Kentuckians) are a good way to notice the year turning. And Brandeis is a reminder that a sharp, careful mind from a river town can reshape the country's thinking.
