Asbury Theological Seminary, in the small town of Wilmore south of Lexington, has announced a new center devoted to how Christian theology speaks to public life.

## What it is

The **Runnels Family Center for Political and Public Theology** is funded by a legacy gift from the family of Gail and Virginia Runnels and also honors their son, David Allen Runnels. Its stated aim is to bring a **Wesleyan** theological perspective (the tradition that grew from John and Charles Wesley's Methodist revival) to questions facing the church and the wider society.

Seminary President David F. Watson named a range of topics where he believes the Wesleyan tradition has something to offer: human dignity, stewardship of resources, immigration, the sanctity of life, human trafficking, religious liberty and the common good. The seminary describes the center as non-partisan.

## What it will do

- Research, white papers and practical resources for churches
- Gatherings that bring together scholars, ministry leaders, policymakers and civic leaders
- Groundwork for future degree programs in public theology

## Who leads it

The first executive director is **Dr. Asa James Swan**, who holds a Ph.D. in modern European history from the University of Kentucky. He was Kentucky's first Chief Leadership Officer and earlier served as chief of staff and legislative liaison at the Kentucky Transportation Cabinet. The center is expected to launch fully in 2027.

## Why it matters

"Public theology" asks how Christian convictions should shape life in a shared, plural society, not just inside church walls. Wesleyans have long joined personal holiness to "social holiness," from prison reform to abolition. A center grounded in that tradition and explicitly non-partisan could be a useful voice at a time when faith and politics are often fused or set at odds.
