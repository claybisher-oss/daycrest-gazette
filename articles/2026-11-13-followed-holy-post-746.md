Episode 746 of The Holy Post (about 1 hour 21 minutes, released November 11) has two halves. This write-up is based on the episode's show notes and chapter list.

## Part one: reading the election

Phil Vischer, Skye Jethani and Kaitlyn Schiess respond to commentators who described the midterm results as a win for the gospel, citing pieces from The Atlantic, Religion News Service and Holy Post's own writers. Their questions are fair to both sides of the aisle:

- Does a vote against one political movement actually mean Christianity is becoming kinder or more faithful?
- Could a growing "religious left" repeat the very mistake critics charged the religious right with, tying the faith to a party's fortunes?

(There's also a light opening segment about an Italian chef's opinion of pineapple pizza.)

## Part two: praying through politics

At about the 46-minute mark, Schiess interviews **Sarah L. Sanderson** about her book *Without Ceasing: Bold Prayers for Troubled Times*, which records a year of praying about public life. Chapters cover praying in the middle of politics, what a prophet actually is (around 57:30), and praying alongside others with curiosity (around 1:08:40).

Sanderson's key observation is that sustained prayer changed her **posture** more than her **positions**: it pushed her toward loving opponents, listening, and seeking God's will humbly rather than claiming God for her side.

## Why it matters

Whatever one's politics, the temptation to make God a mascot for a team is old and bipartisan. A practice of praying for leaders and neighbors, including those we disagree with, is one of the oldest Christian answers to it (1 Timothy 2:1–2).

## Worth watching?

The Sanderson interview, yes; it's the heart of the episode. Skip ahead to about 46:46 if election commentary isn't what you need this week.
