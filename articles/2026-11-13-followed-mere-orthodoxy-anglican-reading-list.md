Joseph Whitenton, an Anglican youth pastor studying at a Reformed seminary, noticed something: his Presbyterian classmates are handed a clear set of classic books, while many Anglicans aren't. His essay for Mere Orthodoxy argues that Anglicanism once had its own such canon and builds a reading list to recover it.

## The argument

Whitenton writes from the "Reformational" wing of Anglicanism, the stream that sees the English Reformation's Protestant convictions as central. He insists this isn't in competition with liturgy, bishops and catholic continuity. Instead, he says, it supplies what liturgical Anglicanism can lack: Scripture-centered devotion, a heartfelt grasp of the gospel, and preaching that presses the text on each listener's conscience. He acknowledges this is a minority view in North American Anglicanism and thinks changes in the global communion make it newly relevant.

## The list, in layers

- **A starter pack of ten:** the Thirty-Nine Articles and the Homilies, Jewel's *Apology*, Nowell's *Catechism*, P.E. Hughes on the English Reformers, Ussher's *Body of Divinity*, Litton and Griffith Thomas on doctrine, Simeon's sermon outlines, Ryle's *Holiness*, Packer's work on the Anglican heritage, and Sibbes's *The Bruised Reed*.
- **Three "guides to further reading":** Ryle's *Knots Untied*, Bickersteth's *The Christian Student* and Bridges's *The Christian Ministry*.
- **A long historical tour:** Cranmer, Bullinger's *Decades*, Vermigli, Latimer, Ridley and Whitaker, then Hooker, Perkins, Davenant, Hall, Preston, Leighton and Pearson, closing with modern studies.

A second part will cover the Evangelical Revival and twentieth-century evangelicals.

## Why it matters

Whatever your tradition, a curated list like this is a gift: it turns "I should read the classics" into a plan. Several titles (Sibbes, Ryle, the Homilies) are short, warm and well suited to slow morning reading. Fittingly, Charles Simeon, on the starter list, is remembered in the Church of England today.

## Worth watching?

Worth bookmarking rather than reading straight through: it's a catalogue more than an argument, and its value is in coming back to it.
