Ryan Holiday met the author Robert Greene (*The 48 Laws of Power*, *Mastery*) when he was 19 and became his research assistant: transcribing interviews, digging through obscure sources and running his website. Holiday says the real pay was the education. To mark Greene's new book, *The Law of the Sublime*, he lists 23 lessons from that long apprenticeship.

## The book behind the post

Greene spent about seven years on the new book. After a stroke in 2018, much of it was written by hand. That alone illustrates several of the lessons.

## The lessons, grouped

**On craft**
- Serious work takes as long as it takes; don't rush it to fit a schedule.
- Sloppy sourcing is unacceptable. Check everything.
- Intuition is built by decades of reading, and creativity is mostly accumulated effort.

**On attention**
- Guard your focus fiercely.
- Don't hand difficult thinking to machines. Greene warned against using AI to translate hard texts, because it skips the struggle that trains the mind. Holiday still keeps research on paper notecards.

**On character**
- Understand manipulation without practicing it; keep a conscience.
- Give credit generously. Take the work seriously, but not yourself.

**On life**
- Keep a physical practice (Greene got Holiday swimming).
- Turn obstacles into material.
- Notice the plain wonder of being alive, and reach out to the people who formed you while you can.

## Why it matters

For anyone learning a language, studying a hard book or writing anything long, the case against shortcuts is the most useful part: the slow work *is* the learning. The notecard habit is easy to borrow.

## Worth watching?

Worth reading if you like Holiday's style; it's list-heavy, but the apprenticeship and research lessons hold up.
