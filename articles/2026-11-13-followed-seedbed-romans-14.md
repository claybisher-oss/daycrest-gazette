J.D. Walt's daily devotional at Seedbed is working slowly through Romans. Friday's entry takes up Romans 14:5–9, where Paul addresses a church split over whether certain days are holier than others and what believers may eat.

## The passage

The Roman church had members who kept particular days as sacred and others who treated every day alike; some abstained from certain foods and some didn't. Walt points out what Paul *doesn't* do: he doesn't negotiate a middle position or rule for one faction. Instead, he tells each person to be fully convinced in their own mind and to do whatever they do "to the Lord." The dispute is relocated: it is no longer a contest between two groups but a question of each person's devotion to Christ.

## Walt's main points

- **Scripture isn't a weapon for winning arguments.** He warns against the habit of reaching for verses mainly to defeat the other side.
- **Beware compromises that run over conscience.** Political-style deal-making can paper over a dispute while ignoring what people actually believe they owe God.
- **The fight on the surface often isn't the real fight.** Many conflicts grow from relational hurt on one or both sides, which is why the issue named rarely gets resolved.
- **Start before God.** Paul grounds everything in the fact that we belong to the Lord in life and in death, because Christ died and rose to be Lord of both. Walt pictures the cross as level ground where estranged people can meet again, and notes that Paul builds this call to unity on eleven chapters about God's mercy.

The entry closes with a short prayer and two journal questions, one of which asks whether a conflict you're in right now is really about what everyone says it's about.

## Why it matters

Churches, families and workplaces all have their "food and holy days" fights. Walt's reading offers a way through that is neither "win" nor "split the difference": take the matter to God first and treat the other person as someone who also answers to the Lord.
