This week's Unbelievable? episode (about an hour, released November 11) isn't a debate. Host John Nelson sits down with Bruce Longenecker of Baylor University, author of *The Materiality of Early Christ Devotion*, to talk about what archaeology can and can't tell us about the first Christians. This write-up is based on the episode's show notes.

## The Pompeii question

Mount Vesuvius buried Pompeii in AD 79, roughly fifty years after the crucifixion. If Christians left any physical trace there, it would be among the earliest material evidence of the movement anywhere. Longenecker discusses the candidates and how scholars weigh them.

## The wider record

From Pompeii the conversation widens to the material world of early Christian devotion:

- **Cross-shaped marks** and whether they were meant as Christian symbols
- **Coded symbols and wordplay** that believers may have used
- **Early church wall paintings**
- **Christian graffiti**, the ordinary scratchings of ordinary believers
- An early image of Jesus that appears in a **magical context**, alongside Egyptian religious imagery

## The big idea

Longenecker's central point is that early devotion to Christ was more varied, and messier, than the neat picture many people carry. Believers expressed their faith surrounded by Roman gods, under the threat of persecution, and next to rival religious practices that sometimes blended into Christian ones.

## Why it matters

For anyone reading through Acts and the Epistles, this fills in the physical world those letters were written into: house churches, city walls, a crowded religious marketplace. It also models careful handling of evidence: what a find can prove, and what it can't.

## Worth watching?

Yes, one of the more substantial episodes of the week if you like early church history or archaeology. It's audio-friendly; no visuals needed.
