The University of Kentucky's Center for Clinical and Translational Science has received a federal grant of almost $34 million from the National Institutes of Health, WEKU reports. A headline piece of the plan is a pilot program meant to make curing Hepatitis C far simpler for people in rural Kentucky.

## What the pilot does

Hepatitis C is a virus that damages the liver over years, often without symptoms. Modern pills can cure most cases, but getting to the pills is the hard part. The usual path runs through several steps (a blood draw, lab work, a follow-up visit, a referral to a specialist) and people drop out at each one, especially where clinics are far apart.

The pilot, called **HEAT** (Hepatitis C Elimination through Accessible Treatment), tries to collapse that chain: test people and begin treatment on the same day, in the places they already go. The first partners are the health departments in **Breathitt, Knott, Lee and Owsley** counties. UK researcher Hannah Knudsen, who presented the plan in Lexington, said the formal launch is still months away.

## What else the money pays for

- Training community health workers
- Developing new researchers
- Building data-science networks for health studies across the state

## The bigger picture

Kentucky has had some of the nation's higher Hepatitis C rates; the CDC counted 5.9 cases per 10,000 Kentuckians in 2023. WEKU notes the award comes at a time when federal research funding overall has been shrinking.

## Why it matters

A cure that exists but is hard to reach doesn't help many people. If a one-visit approach works in the mountains, it could become a model for rural care elsewhere, and it shows Lexington's research university reaching into counties with few clinics.
