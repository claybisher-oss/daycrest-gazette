Diplomacy over the war in Ukraine moved a step this week, though not far. NPR, carrying an Associated Press report, describes three days of meetings across three cities.

## What happened

1. **Abu Dhabi:** U.S.-brokered talks brought Russian and Ukrainian delegations face to face for two days, the first direct talks in nine months. They ended Wednesday with an agreement to swap a large number of prisoners; the exact figure wasn't announced.
2. **Moscow:** On Thursday, U.S. envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner met Russian President Vladimir Putin at the Kremlin for about two hours.
3. **Dublin, next:** The envoys planned to travel on to meet Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy, who was with leaders of more than 40 European countries and NATO's secretary-general.

## Where the sides stand

- **Europe's role:** The U.S. side wants Moscow to accept European participation in talks on broader security. Putin said any European troops in Ukraine after a ceasefire would be treated as targets.
- **Territory:** Russia repeated its claim to four Ukrainian regions it annexed in 2022 but never fully controlled. Zelenskyy welcomed the prisoner exchange but said Ukraine will not give up territory.
- **On the ground:** Fighting continues along a front roughly 750 miles long, with drone and missile strikes reported by both sides.

## Why it matters

Prisoner exchanges are small, real wins: families get people back, and the sides prove they can make a deal. But the core disputes (land and security guarantees) haven't moved, so any ceasefire still looks distant.
