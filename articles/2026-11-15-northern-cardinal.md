The Northern Cardinal (*Cardinalis cardinalis*) stays in Kentucky year-round, and in late fall its red stands out against bare branches.

## How to recognize it
- **Male:** red all over, with a black mask around a thick, cone-shaped red-orange bill and a pointed crest.
- **Female:** warm buff-tan, with red edging on wings, crest and tail and the same bright bill.
- **Sound:** a loud, clear whistled *cheer-cheer-cheer* or *birdie-birdie-birdie*, and a sharp metallic *chip* call.

## Where to look locally
Thickets, hedgerows and woodland edges, along creeks and at backyard feeders, especially those with sunflower seed. Kentucky River bottomlands and farm fencerows are good bets.

## A fun fact
Unlike many songbirds, female cardinals sing too, often from the nest, and mated pairs may trade song phrases back and forth.

## Why it matters
Cardinals are an easy first bird for beginning birders and children: bright, common, loud and present all winter. Kentucky named it the state bird in 1926.
