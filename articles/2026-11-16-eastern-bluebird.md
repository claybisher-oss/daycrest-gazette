## How to know it
A small thrush with a vivid blue back and wings, a rusty-orange throat and breast, and a white belly. Females are grayer with blue in the wings and tail.

## Listen for
A soft, warbled *chur-lee* — often the first clue that a small flock is nearby.

## Where to look
Fence lines, utility wires and lone trees over open pasture and farm fields across the Bluegrass. In winter they gather in small flocks and eat berries such as sumac, dogwood and eastern red cedar.

## Fun fact
Their blue isn't a pigment: it comes from the way tiny structures in the feathers scatter light.

## Why it matters
Bluebirds nearly vanished from much of their range last century; nest-box trails brought them back — a conservation success you can see from the road.
