## Flight disruptions into Monday
The FAA stopped all U.S. flights for more than four hours Friday after its communications network was flooded with connection attempts traced to radar and radio sites in Texas and Arkansas. The agency says there's no sign of a cyberattack. More than 12,000 flights were canceled, and recovery is expected to be gradual. ([NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/11/15/nx-s1-6041683/flight-disruptions-expected-to-last-into-monday) · [FAA statement](https://www.faa.gov/newsroom/faa-statement-nationwide-ground-stop))

## Statehouses after the midterms
After the Nov. 3 elections, Democrats appear on track to control about 24 legislative chambers to Republicans' 22, flipping the Texas House, Georgia House and Pennsylvania Senate. ([NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/11/15/nx-s1-6039175/midterms-legislatures-states-democrats-gop))

## Coal-dust protections
Federal mine regulators have proposed changes that critics say would weaken enforcement of the silica-dust limits adopted in 2024. Black lung has returned in some Appalachian miners at rates last seen in the late 1970s. ([NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/11/15/nx-s1-6039311/laws-limiting-coal-miners-exposure-to-toxic-dust-are-in-danger-of-being-rolled-back))

## Why it matters
The mine-dust rules land hardest in eastern Kentucky's coalfields; watch for the comment period and the lawsuits expected to follow.
