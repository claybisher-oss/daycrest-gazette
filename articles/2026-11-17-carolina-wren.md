## How to know it
![Field-guide card: the five marks to look for](img/2026-11-17-carolina-wren-card.jpg)

Warm cinnamon back, buff belly, a long white eyebrow and a slightly curved bill. It is bigger and brighter than a House Wren, which has already left for the winter. Listen first: the "teakettle" song plus a dry, scolding chatter.

## Where to find it
Any brushy edge in Central Kentucky: backyard shrubs, creek thickets, old fencerows and woodland margins. Brush piles and sheds make favorite winter roosts.

## Fun fact
Hard winters are this bird's limit. After a severe one, numbers at the northern edge of the range can crash, then rebuild over a few mild years — a brush pile in your yard helps.

## Try it today
Step outside for five minutes this morning or at lunch. If you hear a loud three-part phrase repeated fast, look low in the shrubs: a small brown bird with its tail cocked is your wren.

## Why it matters
It's a year-round neighbor you can learn by ear in one morning — maybe the easiest first bird song there is.
