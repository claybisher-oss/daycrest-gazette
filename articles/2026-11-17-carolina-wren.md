## In brief
- Small, round, rusty-brown wren with a bold white eyebrow stripe and a tail often cocked up.
- Huge voice for its size: a loud, rolling "teakettle-teakettle-teakettle," even in winter.
- Pairs stay together year-round and defend the same patch — brush piles, porches, hanging planters.

## How to know it
Warm cinnamon back, buff belly, long white eyebrow, slightly curved bill. It is bigger and brighter than a House Wren, which has already left for the winter. Listen first: the "teakettle" song plus a dry, scolding chatter.

## Where to find it
Any brushy edge in Central Kentucky: backyard shrubs, creek thickets, old fencerows and woodland margins. Brush piles and sheds make favorite winter roosts.

## Fun fact
Hard winters are this bird's limit. After a severe one, numbers at the northern edge of the range can crash, then rebuild over a few mild years — a brush pile in your yard helps.

## Why it matters
It's a year-round neighbor you can learn by ear in one morning — maybe the easiest first bird song there is.
