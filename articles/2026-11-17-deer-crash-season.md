## In brief
- Deer move most from October through December, during mating season.
- Kentucky State Police counted 3,411 deer-related crashes in 2025, a seven-year high; 823 happened in November.
- The safest move when a deer appears is to brake firmly and stay in your lane.

## What's happening
LEX 18 reports that the fall rut puts deer on roads at all hours, but especially around dawn and dusk — exactly when commuters are driving with low light. Rural two-lane roads with field edges and creek crossings are the usual hot spots in Central Kentucky.

## What to do
Slow down where you see deer-crossing signs or wooded edges, use high beams when no one is coming, and remember that deer often travel in groups — if one crosses, expect another. Swerving tends to cause worse crashes than hitting the animal. Always wear a seat belt.

## Why it matters
With short days and dark commutes, the next few weeks carry the year's highest risk. A few seconds of extra caution at dawn and dusk is the simplest protection there is.
