Front Porch Republic ran Dixie Dillon Lane's review (Nov 17) of Peco Gaskovski's *The Last Upheaval*, a prequel to his 2023 novel *Exogenesis*. She expected a tour of backstory and found a book that surprised her.

## The story
Sol Mereman writes for the tech industry and is haunted by a yellow-eyed demon. He and his wife Isbeth follow different paths of Christian commitment: he through private prayer and fasting, she through a church with radical ideas.

## What the reviewer noticed
- **The moderate's bet.** Sol keeps one foot in the technocracy and hopes for a gentle shift, not an upheaval. Neither his family nor the world grants him that.
- **A family story first.** The conflict is relational, and Sol keeps making things worse with his children and his sick wife. Lane calls him "relatable, pitiable, and also sometimes despicable."
- **An open question.** Is the dystopia caused by the tech economy, protesters, disease, government, the demon, or plain human sin? The book makes readers decide.

## Why it matters
Christian fiction that takes technology seriously is rare. Lane's review suggests this one asks the reader to examine his own half-commitments, which is the useful kind of discomfort.

## Worth reading in full?
Yes, if you're looking for your next novel — the review gives enough to decide without spoiling it.
