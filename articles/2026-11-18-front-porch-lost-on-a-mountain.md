In 1939, twelve-year-old Donn Fendler got separated from his party on Mount Katahdin, Maine's highest peak, and spent nine days alone in the woods. S. Dorman retells the story in Front Porch Republic (Nov 16) and calls him a hero.

## The ordeal
Hunger, insects, leeches, cold, sunburn, sickness and injuries that cost him part of a toe — and, near the end, visions he took for real. His own account, *Lost on a Mountain in Maine* (1978), has been read in Maine schools for decades.

## How he survived
- **Follow water downhill.** A Boy Scout rule. Searchers kept to the summit and never came within ten miles of him.
- **Prayer.** What began as habit became necessity once the visions started, and he felt led onward.

## Why it matters
A true survival story with faith at its center, short enough to read aloud. A good one for boys who love the outdoors — and a reminder of a simple rule worth teaching them: if lost, find water and follow it down.

## Worth reading in full?
Yes, and the book itself is a fine family read-aloud.
