People often say the creed of Nicaea turned on a single letter: *homoousios* (same substance) versus *homoiousios* (like in substance). In a Mere Orthodoxy essay (Nov 17), Wyatt Graham argues that story gets the history wrong.

## Four camps
- **Homoousian** — the Son is the same substance as the Father.
- **Homoiousian** — the Son is like the Father in substance.
- **Homoian** — the Son is "like" the Father; avoid substance language.
- **Anomoian** — the Son is unlike the Father.

## What the evidence shows
From about 360, leading pro-Nicenes — Athanasius, Hilary of Poitiers, Basil of Caesarea — treated the homoiousians as fellow believers close to the full Nicene word. Hilary put it this way: "To approve of homoiousion, we need not disapprove of homoousion." One reason for caution about *homoousios*: Marcellus of Ancyra's modalist use of it made many uneasy. The homoians and anomoians opposed both groups, which pushed the two together after 362.

## The real dividing line
Whether one confessed that the Son is eternally from the Father's own essence. That, not a diphthong, was the question.

## Why it matters
It's a corrective to a popular put-down of the creeds, and a model of reading history with patience. Useful background for anyone who confesses the Nicene Creed on Sunday.
