For the 300th anniversary of *Gulliver's Travels* (1726), historian Elizabeth Stice turns in Mere Orthodoxy (Nov 16) to one of its darkest episodes: the Struldbrugs of Luggnagg, people who never die.

## The story
Gulliver is thrilled at first. With endless life he imagines gaining wealth, wisdom and a lifetime of public service. His hosts correct him: the Struldbrugs keep aging. They become peevish, forgetful and envious of the dead. The law even bars them from owning land or testifying in court.

## Stice's points
- **Endless life is not endless youth.** She sets the Struldbrugs beside today's longevity regimens of biomarkers and transfusions.
- **Long life doesn't make good people.** The immortals were no paragons of virtue. In her words, "A prolonged life is not the same thing as a perfectible life."
- **The grave is beaten another way.** Extending the present can't defeat corruption of body and soul.

## Why it matters
A neat bridge between a classic book and the Christian hope of resurrection — not more of this life, but a new one.

Yes, and it may send you back to Swift's Part III.
