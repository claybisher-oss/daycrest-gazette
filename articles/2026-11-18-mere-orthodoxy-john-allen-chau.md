Mere Orthodoxy marked the eighth anniversary of John Allen Chau's death with an essay (Nov 17). Chau, a young American missionary, was killed in November 2018 trying to reach the isolated people of North Sentinel Island in the Indian Ocean.

## The account
The essay retraces his last days from the published reports: the landing, the arrows, his swim back to the fishing boat, and his death the next day. The author holds that Chau went for one reason — the gospel — and expects his death to bear fruit.

## The argument
- **Unwise, yes.** Landing near a people known for violence was not prudent. But those quickest to fault his preparation are not reaching such people themselves. Is doing nothing better?
- **A different reaction.** Public response to Chau was far harsher than the sympathy shown in 1956 for the five missionaries killed in Ecuador. The author reads that as a sign of how evangelism is now viewed.
- **Courage needed.** Those who follow his path will need conviction, and the essay ends in prayer.

## Why it matters
It is a hard case on purpose: zeal, prudence and love of neighbor pulling in different directions. Worth thinking through slowly rather than settling quickly.

## Worth reading in full?
Yes — it's short, and it ends with a line from one of the Ecuador martyrs worth remembering.
