## Phone pouches and test scores
Researchers from Harvard, Dartmouth and NWEA studied schools that lock students' phones in pouches during the day. They found small but real gains in math and reading — roughly one to two weeks of learning a year — at about $25 per student. The study is not yet peer-reviewed, and an earlier peer-reviewed study found almost no effect. [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/11/17/nx-s1-6035684/school-cellphone-ban-learning-loss-yondr-research)

## Iran and the U.S. trade strikes near Bahrain
Bahrain's air defenses shot down Iranian drones aimed at a U.S. radar site Tuesday, Iran's third attack on U.S. targets there in five days after a two-month lull. A U.S. jet then struck a Revolutionary Guard missile site on Qeshm Island. Neither side reported injuries; Qatar warned the fighting could set back peace talks. [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/11/17/nx-s1-6044255/us-iran-war-latest-bahrain)

## LeVar Burton looks back
In a new memoir, the longtime Reading Rainbow host recalls a Sacramento boyhood when he hoped to become a priest, being cast as Kunta Kinte in *Roots* while still in college, his years on Star Trek, and living with a bipolar diagnosis. [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/11/17/nx-s1-6043200/levar-burton-reading-rainbow-star-trek-roots-memoir)
