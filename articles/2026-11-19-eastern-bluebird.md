## How to know it
A thrush a little bigger than a sparrow. Males are bright blue above with a rusty-orange throat and breast; females are grayer with blue in the wings and tail. Watch it perch on a wire or fence post, drop to the grass for an insect, and flutter back up.

## Where to find it
Open fields with scattered trees — farm fence lines, pasture edges, big lawns. In winter it eats berries (dogwood, sumac, holly, cedar) and will visit suet or mealworm feeders.

## Fun fact
Bluebirds aren't blue from pigment. Tiny structures in the feathers scatter blue light — the same trick that makes the sky blue. When their numbers fell last century, volunteers' nest-box trails helped bring them back.

## Why it matters
A bluebird box on a post is one of the easiest ways for a family or a farm to help a native bird — and to watch a nest from eggs to fledglings in spring.
