## Lexington's surplus is spoken for
Most of the city's roughly $6 million projected surplus went to priorities named by the mayor, such as paving and fleet fuel. About $735,000 was left for the council to divide; an effort to strip a $250,000 council capital-projects line failed, and the rest went to seven items including non-profit capital projects, a weatherization pilot and Lexington Children's Theater. [WUKY](https://www.wuky.org/text/wuky-news/2026-11-18/lexingtons-remaining-surplus-budget-dollars-allocated-despite-some-council-debate)

## Affordable homes on Transylvania's old ballfield
State and local officials broke ground at Transylvania University's former baseball field, where 12.5 acres are planned to become more than 200 affordable units, funded through a bank pool of Community Reinvestment Act credits with state support. [WUKY](https://www.wuky.org/text/wuky-news/2026-11-17/transylvania-universitys-old-baseball-field-on-its-way-to-becoming-affordable-housing-site)

## Builders press lawmakers on the housing shortage
Kentucky home builders are asking the General Assembly to cut regulatory and infrastructure barriers. The Kentucky Chamber's "Let Kentucky Build" report projects a shortage of nearly 300,000 units within three years if supply doesn't rise; lawmakers say housing legislation is coming. [LEX 18](https://www.lex18.com/news/covering-kentucky/housing-shortage-lawmakers-nov-18-2026)
