The Holy Post's latest episode, released Wednesday and running about an hour and twenty minutes, covers three linked questions about identity in the church: how Christians speak about God, what the word "evangelical" still names, and what kind of people churches end up forming in their leaders. This summary is based on the episode's detailed show notes and chapter list; no transcript was available.

## God and gender

The main news segment grows out of a social media argument Phil Vischer found himself in over whether God should be described as masculine. With Kaitlyn Schiess and Mike Erre, he looks at what Scripture actually says, why some Christians defend God's masculinity so vigorously, and how the debate is really a proxy for larger disagreements about what men and women are. It is a live question in theology: the Bible's dominant use of masculine names and pronouns for God sits alongside clear teaching that God is spirit and beyond human sex, and traditions weigh those facts differently.

## Does "evangelical" still exist?

The hosts then turn to a Christian Post commentary in which Carl Trueman argues that "evangelical" now covers so much ground that it has nearly stopped meaning anything. That raises a practical problem the show presses on: if the label goes, how would anyone tell apart churches that believe very different things? A shorter segment looks at an Atlantic piece critiquing the politics of the religious left.

## Charisma versus character

In the interview, Skye Jethani talks with therapist and author Chuck DeGroat, known for his earlier book on narcissism in church settings, about his new book, *Who You're Becoming*. The chapters cover how bullying can get dressed up in spiritual language, how performance pressures shape pastoral work, what that costs pastors' own spiritual growth, and a closing reflection on attention and devotion.

## Why it matters

Each segment touches the same nerve: the gap between public image and inner formation. That applies to how we picture God, how churches brand themselves, and how leaders are chosen and shaped. The DeGroat conversation in particular connects with anyone trying to build habits of attention and prayer that form character rather than just output.

## Worth reading in full?

The show notes and linked articles, especially the Trueman piece, are a good starting point, and DeGroat's book looks worth a look.
