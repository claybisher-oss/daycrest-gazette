## A new chief for the Lexington Fire Department
Mayor Linda Gorton announced Thursday that assistant chief Joey Harris will be the next chief of the Lexington Fire Department. Harris grew up in Lexington, has served 25 years in the department, rising through every rank, and has held executive roles since 2020; he also served in the Marine Corps from 1997 to 2001. He follows Chief Jason Wells, who announced in September that he is retiring after 30 years; Wells's retirement takes effect Jan. 10, 2027. [WKYT, Nov. 19](https://www.wkyt.com/2026/11/19/lexington-mayor-announces-new-fire-chief/)

## The city's winter storm plan, explained
After two winters of complaints about snow and ice response, Lexington's plan (unveiled in October) adds more drivers, including licensed employees from other city divisions, and combines Rank 3 and Rank 4 streets so routes run more efficiently. The National Weather Service is the main forecast source, checked against local forecasts. A possible "Plan B" with Fayette County Public Schools would merge neighborhood bus stops onto cleared roads, though the mayor says details are still being worked out. City leaders say treating every street the same isn't possible and that they'll review the plan after the first snowfall. [WKYT Investigates, Nov. 19](https://www.wkyt.com/2026/11/19/lexington-outlines-new-winter-storm-plan-after-two-years-criticism/)

## Why it matters
Both stories are about the city's readiness for hard days: who leads its firefighters, and whether side streets and school routes get cleared when the first real storm arrives.
