## The idea
Every missed shot is a coin flip for the next possession. Offensive rebounding rate measures how often a team wins that flip on its own misses: offensive rebounds divided by all the rebounds available after its missed shots (its offensive boards plus the other team's defensive boards).

## The numbers that matter Friday
A typical college team rebounds somewhere around three of every ten of its own misses. Indiana has been closer to four of ten — about 42%, per Inside the Hall — and those second chances were a big part of its 81–76 win over Arkansas at Madison Square Garden. [Inside the Hall](https://www.insidethehall.com/2026/11/18/five-takeaways-from-iu-basketballs-win-against-arkansas/)

## How a defense fights it
- **Box out first, then go get the ball.** Find your man as the shot goes up and put your body between him and the rim.
- **Guards rebound too.** Long shots make long rebounds that land near the free-throw line.
- **Don't foul on the put-back.** A second-chance basket plus a free throw is the worst outcome.

## Try it tonight (Friday)
Pick five Indiana misses and watch only the rebound: count how many Hoosiers crash the glass and whether a Kentucky player made contact first. If Indiana gets fewer than three of every ten, Kentucky has won the battle that matters most.

## Why it matters
Rebounding rate turns "they outworked us" into something you can see and count — and it is the clearest single key to this game. Tip-off is Friday night at Lucas Oil Stadium on CBS.
