## The ruling
The International Court of Justice in The Hague — the U.N.'s top court — ruled today in the case accusing Myanmar of genocide against the Rohingya, a mostly Muslim minority. NPR's report is headlined that the court ruled Myanmar committed genocide. The full reasoning and the judges' vote are in the original report. [NPR, Nov. 19](https://www.npr.org/2026/11/19/g-s1-154340/un-court-myanmar-genocide-rohingya)

## How we got here
Gambia brought the case in 2019 over the Myanmar military's 2017 campaign in Rakhine State, which drove hundreds of thousands of Rohingya into Bangladesh. In 2020 the court ordered Myanmar to take steps to prevent genocide and later rejected Myanmar's challenge to its jurisdiction; hearings on the merits were held in January 2026. Myanmar has denied genocide, saying it was responding to an insurgent uprising. Gambia asked for reparations and the safe return of refugees. [Al-Monitor/Reuters preview](https://www.al-monitor.com/originals/2026/11/top-un-court-ruling-myanmar-genocide-be-watched-clues-gaza) · [Irrawaddy/AFP background](https://www.irrawaddy.com/news/myanmars-crisis-the-world/top-un-court-to-rule-november-19-on-myanmar-rohingya-genocide-case.html)

## Why it matters
It is the court's first substantive ruling under the 1948 Genocide Convention in more than a decade, and lawyers expect it to shape how other genocide cases before the court are argued. The court has no police of its own, so what follows depends on states and the U.N. Security Council.
