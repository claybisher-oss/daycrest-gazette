## What it is
Daily Dose of Greek (now part of Daily Dose of Scripture) posts one short video per weekday: a single New Testament verse read aloud in Greek, then taken apart word by word — forms, parsing, and a few notes on how the grammar shapes the meaning. The series has been working verse by verse through Matthew 23, and the newest lesson (posted Nov. 19) is **Matthew 23:34**. ## The verse in context
Matthew 23 is Jesus' long indictment of the scribes and Pharisees — the seven "woes." Verse 34 turns from accusation to announcement. It opens with *dia touto* ("therefore," literally "because of this") and *idou* ("look!"), then a present-tense verb used with a future sense: *egō apostellō* — "I am sending." Notice that Jesus himself is the sender: he sends prophets, wise men and scribes, and says some will be killed and crucified, flogged in synagogues and pursued from town to town. The verse sets up verse 35's sweep from Abel to Zechariah and the lament over Jerusalem at the end of the chapter (vv. 37–39).

## Why it matters for a learner
Even before you can read a full sentence, a verse like this is full of words you'll meet constantly: *dia* (through/because of), *idou* (behold), *egō* (I), *pros* (to/toward), *kai* (and). Watching someone read slowly, at real speed and then broken down, trains the ear and the eye together — the same recognize → recall → use loop the pages use.
