## How to know it
A sparrow-sized bird in a tidy gray hood: slate-gray head, back and breast, a sharp line to a clean white belly, and a small pale-pink bill. The giveaway comes when it flushes from a path or lawn — two bright white outer tail feathers flash, then fold away.

## Listen
The song is a dry, steady trill on one pitch, a little like a sewing machine. In winter you'll more often hear a sharp "tick" or "smack" call as a flock feeds.

## Where to find it here
Juncos nest in northern and mountain forests and come south for winter, which is why many people call them "snowbirds." In central Kentucky look on the ground under feeders, along hedgerows and at woodland edges — Shaker Village's trails, the edges of Asbury's lawns and Wilmore's backyards all work. They usually travel in small flocks, often with sparrows.

## Fun fact
"Dark-eyed Junco" is one species with several looks across North America; the gray "slate-colored" form is the one we get in the East.
