## What the coaches said
**Will Stein (Kentucky, first season)** called the recent scoring drop mostly self-inflicted — Kentucky has 20 points in its last two games combined — and expects a hungrier, sharper Missouri after two losses; the winner, he said, will be the team that's more physical and more connected. He also said the Cats' physicality fell short against Florida, when they ran 27 times for 61 yards, a season low. ## Learn one thing: the line of scrimmage
![Diagram of an offense lined up at the line of scrimmage: tackle, guard, center, guard, tackle in the middle](img/2026-11-21-line-of-scrimmage.svg)
*Diagram: Daycrest Gazette*

Before every snap the ball sits on an imaginary line across the field — the line of scrimmage. The offense must have at least seven players on that line. The five in the middle — two tackles, two guards and the center — are the offensive line: they block for the run and protect the quarterback, and they are not allowed to catch forward passes. Tonight that matters more than usual: Kentucky ran for just 61 yards last week, and Missouri allows only about 104 rushing yards a game.

**Try it tonight:** on Kentucky's first three running plays, watch only the offensive line for the first two seconds. Is the line of scrimmage moving forward (Missouri pushed back) or backward? Write down which.

## Why it matters
A win steadies a season that was sliding; a third straight loss would send Kentucky into the Louisville game with real questions. The line of scrimmage will likely tell the story early.
