Mere Orthodoxy's editor Jake Meador wrote this week about his grandfather, Robert Meador, who died at 88.

## The man
The piece opens at a graveside in Omaha: Robert, in serious pain in his feet and ankles, refused a chair and stood behind his wife because she needed him there. From there Meador traces an ordinary, hard-working life — adoption, warehouse jobs, driving an armored truck, raising and butchering animals in the backyard, a family trip to Disneyland in a Buick towing a pop-up camper. Raised Lutheran with Norwegian roots, Robert became Catholic to marry the woman he stayed with for 65 years; she died late last year.

## Coming when called
Two rescue stories anchor the essay. Robert once pulled his son's jeep out of Platte River mud with two friends and three pickups. Years later, Jake's own father came out on a snowy road for him — because his father had always come for him. Meador notes that these stories open his book *What Are Christians For?* and suggests his grandfather was its real hero.

## Why it matters
Meador doesn't romanticize the working-class Catholic world his grandfather lived in; he names its failings. But he sees real goodness in a man who counted himself expendable next to his family and whose pain came from work worth doing. It is a quiet portrait of what faithfulness can look like over a lifetime.
