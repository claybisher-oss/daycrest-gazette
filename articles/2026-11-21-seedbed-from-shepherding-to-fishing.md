Friday's Wake-Up Call keeps walking through the end of Romans. The text is Romans 15:7–13, where Paul tells the Roman believers to accept one another as Christ has accepted them, and then strings together Old Testament promises that the Gentiles will praise God.

## Shepherds and nets
J.D. Walt frames the whole Bible with two pictures. The Old Testament, he says, is largely a shepherding story: God gathering and keeping a flock. The New Testament turns into a great fishing expedition, as God draws people from every nation. And when Jesus calls his disciples "fishers of people," Walt points out, he means net fishing, not a lone angler with a rod and bait.

## A net made of knots
That detail carries his argument. A net is only as strong as its knots, and Walt reads the knots as covenant friendships — people bound to one another the way Jesus bound his friends. People are "caught" not by a clever lure but by being drawn into those relationships. He suggests the net is the overlooked symbol of the church, and reads Paul's letter to Rome as an effort to retie knots that had frayed between Jewish and Gentile believers.

## The bait problem
Walt is blunt that many churches have spent a century perfecting bait fishing — attractions to bring people in. His alternative is slower: small groups of friends who band together and go where people already live. Relationships, he says, are the mission.

## Why it matters
For a week that has been about Romans 5's "much more," this is a practical landing: the gift that outweighs the fall makes a people, and the people are held together by ordinary, mended friendships.
