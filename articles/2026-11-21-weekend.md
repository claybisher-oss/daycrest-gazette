Three pieces worth a slow read this weekend — each with our write-up and the original.

## 1. "Church Age Mindset" (Mere Orthodoxy)
Sacha Mugisha's answer to the online cult of strength: real vitality isn't seized by domination but received from the Holy Spirit through self-giving, patterned on Christ's own dying and rising. A good companion to Christ the King Sunday — a King whose power looks like a cross. About 15 minutes. [Original](https://mereorthodoxy.com/church-age-mindset/) · [Our write-up](https://claybisher-oss.github.io/daycrest-gazette/a/2026-11-20-noon-followed-mere-orthodoxy.html)

## 2. "Tending Neglected Rural Places" (Front Porch Republic)
Hadden Turner reviews Charles Cotherman's *Strip Mine Jesus*, a plea for Christians to commit long-term to a place — or move toward the small towns the economy has passed by — and work alongside the people already there. It names three idols (celebrity, over-efficiency, autonomy) and argues that attention comes before affection. About 12 minutes. [Original](https://www.frontporchrepublic.com/2026/11/tending-neglected-rural-places/) · [Our write-up](https://claybisher-oss.github.io/daycrest-gazette/a/2026-11-21-front-porch-republic-tending-rural-places.html)

## 3. "In Memoriam: Robert Meador (1938–2026)" (Mere Orthodoxy)
Jake Meador remembers his grandfather — warehouse worker, armored-truck driver, backyard farmer, husband of 65 years — whose costly, unglamorous faithfulness was the quiet hero of Meador's own book. A piece about what goodness looks like in an ordinary life. About 10 minutes. [Original](https://mereorthodoxy.com/in-memoriam-robert-meador-1938-2026/) · [Our write-up](https://claybisher-oss.github.io/daycrest-gazette/a/2026-11-21-mere-orthodoxy-robert-meador.html)

## Why it matters
All three are about the same thing from different angles: faithfulness that stays put and gives itself away.
